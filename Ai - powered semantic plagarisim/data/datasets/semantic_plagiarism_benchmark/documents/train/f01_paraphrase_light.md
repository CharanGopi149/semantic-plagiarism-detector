# Reworded Review of Adaptive Learning Analytics

## Introduction
In practice, higher education institutions increasingly use learning analytics dashboards to identify students who are falling behind before final exams. Teaching teams combine quiz history, attendance patterns, and discussion activity to assess which learners require timely support. Within the case study, this approach is useful in large classes where individual mentoring time is limited.

## Methodology
The pilot platform gathered weekly assessment scores, login frequency, and assignment submission gaps from the course platform. In practice, a risk model clustered students into low, medium, and high-support categories and refreshed the label after each new activity. Advisors checked the model output every Friday and sent focused study guidance to students with rising risk scores.

## Results
Within the case study, students who were contacted early completed more practice activities and were less likely to miss the next assignment deadline. Faculty reported that the dashboard helped them prioritize intervention without reading every raw activity log. In practice, the strongest gains appeared when automated alerts were combined with short human feedback sessions.

## Conclusion
Learning analytics is most effective when institutions treat prediction as a support tool rather than a punishment mechanism. Within the case study, transparent communication about data use increases student trust and improves participation in advising programs. Future rollouts should monitor bias and ensure that high-risk labels trigger constructive help.
